Graphic Artist, Design Editor

Financial Times, London & Hong Kong, 1998 - 2007

I joined the Financial Times in London in 1998 as a Graphic Artist, creating editorial graphics, illustrations and page designs for one of the world's most respected newspapers. It was an environment where accuracy, clarity and visual restraint were held to exceptionally high standards, and it fundamentally shaped my approach to editorial design.

In 2003, I relocated to Hong Kong to become Design Editor, Asia, taking responsibility for all editorial design produced by the bureau. The role expanded well beyond newspaper production, encompassing everything from daily pages and ft.com to magazines, special reports and bespoke publications for readers across the Asia-Pacific region. Working across multiple editions, cultures and time zones demanded a collaborative approach and the ability to deliver consistently under tight daily deadlines.

Returning to London in 2006, I rejoined the Graphics Desk with a broader perspective and a greater emphasis on innovation. Alongside day-to-day editorial production, I continued developing new products and visual storytelling approaches inspired by the work undertaken during my time in Hong Kong. The experience of working in two global newsrooms gave me an international perspective that has continued to influence my work throughout my career.

Moments That Shaped Me

The Friday Phone Call

Sometimes the biggest opportunities come without warning.

It was late on a Friday afternoon when the phone rang. Gemini News Service needed someone to start on Monday. The designer they'd hired hadn't worked out, and suddenly there was an opportunity I hadn't expected.

Fresh out of college, I had enthusiasm, a portfolio and a willingness to learn. I said yes.

Those first weeks were an intense introduction to newspaper publishing. Every illustration, infographic and portrait was drawn by hand, often against unforgiving weekly deadlines. There was no time to overthink or second-guess myself—you simply got on with the job.

Looking back, it was the perfect start to my career. It taught me that opportunities rarely arrive when you feel completely ready. Sometimes the best thing you can do is simply say yes.

Saying 'Yes' to Hong Kong

Moving from London to Hong Kong wasn't simply a new job, it was a chance to help establish the Financial Times' design and graphics hub for Asia.

It meant launching new publications, working across international editions and taking a senior newsroom role for the first time. It also reinforced something I've discovered throughout my career: the biggest opportunities usually begin with stepping outside your comfort zone.

In at the Deep End

While working for the Financial Times in Hong Kong, I was asked to travel to Shanghai to work with International Financial News as part of a joint venture.

What I thought would be a simple meeting quickly turned into standing in front of a newsroom of around thirty editorila staff, giving an impromptu presentation with nopreparation.

It was daunting, but it taught me another valuable lesson: confidence often comes after you begin, not before. I was later invited back, and the experience gave me the confidence to speak, present ideas and lead creative teams throughout the rest of my career.

Standing by My Principles

When ownership changed at one organisation, I was asked to report back on journalists rather than support them. It wasn't something I believed was right, so I chose to leave.

It wasn't an easy decision, but careers are shaped as much by the choices you don't make as the ones you do. Looking back, I'm still comfortable with that decision.

Starting Again

Learning never really stops.

After more than three decades in editorial design, it would have been easy to rely on the skills I'd already developed. Instead, I found myself drawn to something completely new: interactive storytelling.

With the help of AI,I started teaching myself HTML, CSS and JavaScript, then explored data visualisation, motion design, Three.js and Blender. It reminded me what it feels like to be a beginner again—frustrating at times, but also incredibly rewarding.

The technology continues to evolve, and so do I. Every new skill becomes another way to help people understand complex stories, and another reminder that curiosity is one of the most valuable tools a designer can have.

Home

After years of moving countries, changing jobs and embracing new opportunities, one thing has remained constant: my wife, Julia.

Together we've built a life across four countries and raised three incredible children, each born on a different continent. Looking back, it's a reminder that while careers evolve, the people who share the journey matter most.
